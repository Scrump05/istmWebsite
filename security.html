Article Section: The Morris Worm

The first cyber security hacking in history occurred on November 2, 1988. It was called the Morris Worm and was caused by an attack from a MIT computer by a graduate student named Robert Tappan Morris. A worm is a tool used in hacking which unlike computer viruses, don't need a software host and can grow on their own. This worm soon affected many prestigious universities such as Berkely, Harvard and Princeton, NASA and research facilities on the national electronic network. The worm started by targeting computers that were operated on a specific version of the Unix operating system but quickly spread since it had multiple vectors to attack. Soon after, the one responsible for the worm made an anonymous announcement to the public saying that the worm was meant to be an enclosed experiment and was never meant to get out and create such damage to others. After the announcement, one of Morris friends released to the media that they knew who was responsible for the worm and his name was released to the public. Soon after, the FBI launched an investigation and it led to them finding out that Morris created the worm to slowly and secretly spread all over the internet. Additionally, further information found out that Morris was actually a student from Cornell, but to hide his location, he hacked into an MIT computer from a computer terminal in New York. After the investigation, Morris was tried in court and the court decided that he violated the 1986 law of Computer Fraud Abuse Act, but instead of putting Morris in jail, they instead made him pay a fine, be on probation, and have community service.

This case was significant to cybersecurity history because it caused the U.S. government to create the first computer emergency response team after noticing that they had no way to protect their systems. Additionally, this case created more hacking incidents and motivated younger generations to be malicious hackers.

Types of Malware

  1. Virus: This is a type of software written with malicious intent to cause annoyance or damage to those who encounter it.
  2. Trojan Horse: This is a harmful code that hides inside another software and usually is formatted as an attachment or downloadable file.

Common Hacker Motives

  1. Financial Gain: This type of motivation involves using malware to access data from the recipients system to gain access to their finacial data or to demand funds from them.
  2. Hacktivism: This type of motivation involves hacks that are motivated by political or social causes to attack systems and networks.

Prevention Strategies

  • Knowledge of Phishing Emails: This involves not clicking on suspicious links or downloading attachments from unknown sources.
  • Unique Passwords: To make this the user can create an 8-12 character password with a mix of letters, numbers, and symbols.
