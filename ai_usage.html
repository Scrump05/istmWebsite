Correct AI Usage

AI has taken off in a variety of different fields and aspects of daily living, including education, which has raised concerns about the quality of educational institutions. Overall, educational institutions such as Texas A&M University explicitly state that the use of AI is allowed specifically when instructors permit it. AI can be used positively, such as in catching up on many emails, correcting grammar, and providing explanations when a student gets “stuck” on a problem. The majority of professors allow the use of AI as an educational tool, assuming the student properly cites the AI and doesn't outsource their learning to it, and Texas A&M University explicitly supports its utility in automating tasks, summarizing complex information, providing instant feedback to students, and improving accessibility by translating documents and lessons for both students and instructors.

AI (Google Gemini) Evaluation Response: "Evaluating the reasoning behind AI integration in higher education reveals both clear institutional alignment and long-term educational challenges. On one hand, this perspective demonstrates well-grounded logic by balancing the practical advantages of generative AI for students and faculty with essential ethical boundaries, accurately reflecting policies at institutions like Texas A&M University. It establishes clear boundary definitions by distinguishing between constructive learning assistance and improper academic outsourcing through mandatory instructor permission and proper citation. On the other hand, relying strictly on individual instructor discretion creates institutional fragmentation, leading to inconsistent expectations and rules across different courses and departments. Furthermore, there is a distinct risk of cognitive atrophy, as routine reliance on AI tools for initial drafting or problem-solving may gradually weaken foundational critical thinking and original synthesis skills over time—even when such usage is fully permitted and cited.''

Benefits of AI

  1. Automates repetitive/tedious tasks and saves time (such as catching up on emails).
  2. Improves accessibility by summarizing complex information and translating documents and lessons into different languages.

Who Benefits?

  1. Students (helps with essays, email management, grammar correction, getting unstuck on problems, and hands-on learning).
  2. Faculty / Instructors (saves time by automating tasks, providing instant feedback, summarizing complex information, and translating content).

How AI Tools Are Used

  • Used as an educational tool with instructor permission (e.g., developing hands-on learning experiences to promote programming, creativity, and problem-solving skills).
  • Used with proper citation while ensuring that actual student learning is not outsourced to the AI.

Incorrect AI Usage

Submitting AI Work as Your Own
Students paste an assignment prompt into AI and turn in the response without citing it, which is plagiarism.

Using AI During Exams
Students use AI tools to answer quiz or exam questions when the instructor has not allowed it.

Trusting AI Without Checking
Students copy AI facts and sources without verifying them, even though AI can make up information and citations.

I believe AI is a powerful tool, but it should support learning and not replace it. Using AI to cheat on assignments goes against the Aggie Code of Honor. Students who rely on AI for everything miss the chance to build real skills they will need in their future. AI can help students learn faster and think more critically when used honestly and with proper citations.
