Correct AI Usage

AI has taken off in a variety of different fields and aspects of daily living including education. Many college students use AI to help write essays and to do tedious tasks. But this raises ethical questions specifically in ensuring the quality of education received and credibility of an educational institution. Overall educational institutions such as Texas A&M University explicitly state that the usage of AI is allowed specifically when instructors permit it. AI can be used positively such as in catching up on many emails, correcting grammar, and providing explanations when a student gets “stuck” on a problem. In fact, the official TAMU website explicitly states, “In academia, generative AI can be used to develop hands-on learning experiences that promote programming, creativity, and problem-solving skills”. Generally, I have found that as long as the instructor explicitly permits AI, the student successfully cites the AI used, and the students learning is not outsourced to AI most professors permit using Artificial Intelligence as an educational tool. Regarding the teaching aspect of academia TAMU policy states “Generative AI can help faculty save time by automating repetitive tasks, summarizing complex information, providing instant feedback to students, and improving accessibility by translating documents and lessons into different languages”.

Benefits of AI

  1. Automates repetitive/tedious tasks and saves time (such as catching up on emails).
  2. Improves accessibility by summarizing complex information and translating documents and lessons into different languages.

Who Benefits?

  1. Students (helps with essays, email management, grammar correction, getting unstuck on problems, and hands-on learning).
  2. Faculty / Instructors (saves time by automating tasks, providing instant feedback, summarizing complex information, and translating content).

How AI Tools Are Used

  • Used as an educational tool with instructor permission (e.g., developing hands-on learning experiences to promote programming, creativity, and problem-solving skills).
  • Used with proper citation while ensuring that actual student learning is not outsourced to the AI.
